# ConcurrentPriorityQueue — Throughput, Latency, Stickiness, and ESA-2021 Optimizations (Xeon 8573C, 64 threads)

**Date:** 2026-06-15

The reference performance record for the Bifrost `ConcurrentPriorityQueue` (relaxed MultiQueue) on a
64-thread Xeon 8573C. It covers two things measured in one session on one machine so the numbers are
mutually consistent: (1) the **baseline** — throughput, single-thread latency, and stickiness of the
relaxed queue against a Naive single-lock priority queue and its own strict `TryDequeueMin` scan path
across four workloads; and (2) the **ESA-2021 parity optimizations** that have landed since the port —
the transition-occupancy bitmask (sparse routing), per-sub-queue buffering, and an internal-heap arity
A/B — each measured before/after on the same silicon.

The relaxed queue scales monotonically to about **118 M ops/s at 64 threads** while the single-lock
baseline stays flat near 5–8 M ops/s, a **12–25×** gap. Single-thread latency stays within 1.4–2.2× of
a lock-wrapped `PriorityQueue` at zero allocation, and the stickiness knob recovers most of the relaxed
queue's deficit in the low-thread regime. Of the optimizations, the bitmask delivers a large
sparse-regime win that **widens with `n`** (1.81× at this host's n=256), buffering is a workload-
dependent trade (a low-thread/drain/reference-element win, roughly neutral on high-contention dense
mixed), and the arity A/B is a clean latency-vs-throughput trade — all at `0 B/op`.

## ESA-2021 parity optimizations

Each optimization is measured before/after on this same host so the deltas are not confounded by
hardware. Raw data lives under [`data/azure-d64sv6-2026-06-13/ab/`](data/azure-d64sv6-2026-06-13/ab/).

### Occupancy bitmask — sparse-routing win, confirmed to widen with `n`

The transition-occupancy bitmask (feature `cpq-transition-bitmask`, [design](../designs/2026-06-14-cpq-transition-bitmask.md))
adds a packed `ulong[]` occupancy word set/cleared only on a sub-queue's emptiness-boundary crossings,
plus a `TrailingZeroCount` sparse-routing fallback before the O(n) verification scan. The local i9
before/after (n=128) could only confirm the *direction* — its "before" sparse pair was already ~92 ns,
too shallow to provoke the full O(n)-scan cliff, so the win read as a modest 1.16×. At this host's
**n=256** the cliff is real and large.

**Single-thread Enqueue+Dequeue pair latency** (relaxed `int`, BDN `--job Short`, `0 B/op`):
before = `74a95bb` (no bitmask), after = `main`.

| Population | before (ns) | after (ns) | Δ |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **10** (sparse, ~96% empty) | **264.2** | **145.6** | **1.81× / −44.9%** |
| 1 000 (steady) | 77.3 | 78.3 | +1.3% (noise) |
| 100 000 | 118.9 | 122.3 | +2.9% (noise) |
| 1 000 000 | 167.9 | 170.4 | +1.5% (noise) |

**Dense throughput** (relaxed, M ops/s) confirms no regression off the sparse path:

| Workload | Threads | before | after | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| UniformMixed5050 | 4 | 16.31 | 17.26 | +5.8% |
| UniformMixed5050 | 64 | 113.82 | 118.32 | +3.9% |
| NarrowKeyRange | 64 | 115.99 | 116.55 | +0.5% |
| Drain | 64 | 62.21 | 62.56 | +0.6% |

> **Verdict — confirmed.** The bitmask's sparse win **scales with `n`** exactly as the spike
> predicted: 1.16× at n=128 (i9) → **1.81× (−45%) at n=256** (Xeon), `0 B/op`, with steady-state
> latency within noise and dense throughput after ≥ before. The earlier i9 "−5% at 4T" worry does not
> reproduce here either (UniformMixed-4T is **+5.8%**). The win grows with core count because `n` does.

### Per-sub-queue buffering (ESA §4) — a workload-dependent trade, not a universal win

Buffering (feature `cpq-buffered-multiqueue`, [design](../designs/2026-06-15-cpq-buffered-multiqueue.md);
[GO spike](../research/2026-06-15-cpq-buffering-spike.md)) adds a 16-slot insertion buffer and a sorted
16-slot deletion buffer per sub-queue, touching the deep heap only once per ~16 ops. It ships **opt-in,
default off** (`bufferCapacity = 0`); this A/B toggles the dial between `0` and `16` (the paper optimum
`C`).

**Dense throughput** (relaxed, M ops/s), representative cells:

| Workload | Threads | C=0 | C=16 | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| UniformMixed5050 | 1 | 12.47 | 14.85 | **+19.1%** |
| UniformMixed5050 | 8 | 28.71 | 31.36 | +9.2% |
| UniformMixed5050 | 64 | 114.44 | 110.33 | −3.6% |
| NarrowKeyRange | 1 | 14.99 | 18.51 | **+23.5%** |
| NarrowKeyRange | 64 | 116.94 | 111.36 | −4.8% |
| Drain | 8 | 22.47 | 24.97 | +11.1% |
| Drain | 32 | 49.97 | 53.34 | +6.7% |
| Drain | 64 | 66.75 | 73.10 | **+9.5%** |

**Single-thread pair latency** (BDN `CpqBufferedAb`, `0 B/op`), where buffering's deep-heap
amortization shows its true shape — it pays where element comparisons/moves are expensive (reference
elements), and is roughly neutral for cheap `int`:

| Element | Population | C=0 (ns) | C=16 (ns) | Δ |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **reference** (`string`,`long`) | 100 000 | 150.2 | 121.6 | **−19.0%** |
| **reference** (`string`,`long`) | 1 000 000 | 178.6 | 122.4 | **−31.5%** |
| value (`int`,`int`) | 100 000 | 119.9 | 130.2 | +8.6% |
| value (`int`,`int`) | 1 000 000 | 174.2 | 179.6 | +3.1% |

**Allocation gate:** every cell above — both `C` values, both element types, all populations — is
`0 B/op`. The `[InlineArray(16)]` buffers add no per-op allocation.

> **Verdict — keep default-off; enable per workload.** Buffering is a clear win at **low thread
> counts** (+8–23% at 1–8T, the per-op-floor regime), on the **drain** workload at scale (+9.5% at
> 64T), and for **reference-element** deep heaps (−31% pair latency at 1M — the costly-comparison case
> the deletion buffer is built to amortize). It is roughly **neutral-to-slightly-negative on dense
> mixed at high contention** (−1 to −5% at 32–64T) and neutral for cheap `int`. This matches the spike:
> buffering raises the per-op floor and shortens the critical section rather than lifting the
> coherence-bound dense ceiling. `bufferCapacity=16` is the right knob to expose; default-off remains
> correct, and the default-on question is workload-specific, not universal.

### Internal-heap arity (ESA §6 / [#27](https://github.com/lvlup-sw/bifrost/issues/27)) — a latency-vs-throughput trade

The [#27 spike](../research/2026-06-15-cpq-batched-bulk-heap-spike.md) closed the *specialized
batch-aware internal PQ* (merging heap / Floyd-refill) as NO-GO, leaving one empirical question for this
host: the heap **arity**. Bifrost runs an arity-4 implicit heap (a deliberate write-barrier choice over
the reference impl's arity-8). This A/B patches the heap to arity-8 (throwaway build, gated on the
concurrency correctness suite — **123/123 pass**) and measures it against arity-4 (`main`) on the
value-type `int` path.

| Metric | Regime | arity-4 | arity-8 | Δ |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| single-thread pair latency | pop 1M | 170.4 ns | 182.2 ns | **+6.9% (arity-4 better)** |
| single-thread pair latency | pop 100k | 122.3 ns | 129.9 ns | +6.2% (arity-4 better) |
| dense throughput | UniformMixed 16T | 50.29 | 55.28 | **+9.9% (arity-8 better)** |
| dense throughput | UniformMixed 64T | 118.32 | 122.77 | +3.8% (arity-8 better) |
| dense throughput | NarrowKey 16T | 53.84 | 58.74 | +9.1% (arity-8 better) |
| dense throughput | NarrowKey 64T | 116.55 | 122.59 | +5.2% (arity-8 better) |

> **Verdict — arity-4 stays the default; arity-8 is a real follow-up for value-type throughput.** The
> two regimes pull opposite ways: arity-4 wins **single-thread latency** (fewer comparisons per op,
> +2–7%), arity-8 wins **contended dense throughput at 16–64T** (+4–10% — a shorter `log₈n` critical
> section, and 8 `(int,int)` slots ≈ one 64-byte cache line, which is the paper's exact `k=8`
> rationale realised on server silicon). This **nuances rather than overturns** #27: the value-type
> int harness has no GC write barriers, so it cannot see the per-move barrier cost that scales with
> arity and motivated arity-4 for reference elements. arity-4 remains the right default (latency path +
> reference-element safety); a proper pooled, **reference-element** arity sweep before any change is the
> recommended follow-up. (Numbers are single 3 s windows; 1T and Drain-64T cells carry run-to-run noise
> of a few percent.)
